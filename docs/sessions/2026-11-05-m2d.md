## Adopted behavior

Every 30 ticks, after that day's simultaneous growth: income is $100 grant + $2 per serviced resident + $4 per serviced job. Expenses are $1 per road + $60 per station + $10 per park, including unused/disconnected facilities. Both road access and allocated power are required for tax; unserved standing development pays nothing during decline grace. First settlement displays on day 31. The budget shows current counts/forecast, last actual settlement, opening/closing cash, shortfall waived, and capped surplus.

Cash stays within zero and the existing maximum. Unpaid operating costs are explicitly waived, without debt, arrears, service cuts or destruction. A negative net remains a deficit while reserves cover it. Paid construction still needs full cash; free clear and identical repaint work at zero. Clear surplus facilities and/or restore service to recover. With everything cleared, the $100/month grant can finance another opening.

The pocket park costs $100 and $10/month, occupies one land tile, and cannot block the outside-link marker. Homes within Manhattan distance 3 reserve existing growth demand first; overlap does not stack. Ties remain row-major within covered/uncovered groups, and businesses remain row-major. All prior service requirements, prior-state demand, eight-per-type round caps, one-level-per-round limit, max level 2 and decline rules remain. Parks need no services and relay neither access nor power. They add no demand, capacity, tax multiplier or decline protection. Key 8 selects the park; B opens the modal budget. Tree/path/bench graphics are original code-drawn placeholders.

Schema **4**, simulation/content **m2d-1**, stores a validated `last_budget` input record. Outputs, forecasts and coverage are derived. Schema 3 retains its development/loss arrays; schemas 1/2 gain zeros; all older schemas retain earlier authoritative values and gain empty history. No retroactive settlement. Loading never writes. Older primary/backup bytes are preserved as `manual.schema1/2/3.json` before replacement; preservation failures abort replacement.
